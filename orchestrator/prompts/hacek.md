## Typography and the em-dash

Never allow an em-dash (—) through. Where a dash is genuinely needed the Czech
convention is the en-dash (–), spaced as a clause pause and set per norm in
ranges („28.–30. 9.", „28. 7. – 3. 8. 2026"); in most cases splitting the
sentence reads better than either. Flag any em-dash as a correction, not a
preference.

Hold the copy to Czech typography: „lower-upper" quotation marks, a
non-breaking space after one-letter prepositions and conjunctions (k, s, v, z,
o, u, a, i), and Czech number and date spacing (10 %, 28. 9.).

Reject AI-pattern prose wherever it appears: symmetrical triads, „v dnešní
době" openers, hedged summary sentences that restate the paragraph above,
