# Product marketing context — GoVIRAL

Drafted 2026-08-06 from `config/ventures.json`, `config/goviral-sources.json` and
`docs/GOVIRAL-PHASE2-PROMPT.md`. It exists so the vendored marketing skills stop asking for
foundations this repository already holds. **Nothing here is a claim about performance** — the
venture was founded today and has produced nothing yet.

Document version: 1.0 · Changelog: 1.0 (2026-08-06) — first draft, written at founding.

## 1. Product overview

An internal weekly room, not a product with customers. Every Monday it reads public Instagram and
Threads data, says what is genuinely rising, and turns that into a content brief for the owner and
marketing ideas for the two magazines.

- Category: internal function. Nothing is sold and nothing is published.
- Business model: none, and none planned. It exists to make the other ventures' publishing better.
- Cost: about $0.05–0.06 of model spend a week, plus $0 cash — the Apify Free plan's $5 monthly
  credit and a set of keyless feeds.

## 2. Target audience

Two readers, both internal:

1. **The owner**, who wants to know what to write this week and where to put it.
2. **The magazine desks** (DNESKAi, MMA Files, and Titty Tuesdays when a trend touches it), who
   get trend signals as a tiebreaker between equally sourced candidates.

The job the owner hires it for: *tell me what is worth writing about this week, with the numbers,
so I am not guessing.*

## 3. Personas

Not B2B. One owner, three desks.

## 4. Problems and pain points

Deciding what to publish is either guesswork or an hour of scrolling, and scrolling is the worst
available research method: it surfaces what already peaked. Meanwhile a trend that is genuinely
rising is invisible until it is over.

## 5. Competitive landscape

Nothing to compete with — this is internal. The alternatives are: guess; scroll; or pay for a
social listening tool, which starts around $16/mo for Firecrawl and $50 minimum for DataForSEO,
either of which alone would consume half the $30 all-in operating cap.

## 6. Differentiation

Not applicable in a market sense. What makes it useful rather than decorative:

- **Velocity beats volume.** The score is engagement per hour since posting, so a small rising
  trend outranks a large peaked one. Scrolling gets this exactly backwards.
- **Every call carries its number and its ref.** A trend claim without both is dropped.
- **Skipping is an output.** Most weeks some trends are deliberately not chased, and the reason is
  written down.

## 7. Objections and anti-personas

- *"Is this scraping legal?"* — Logged-out public data only, no login and no cookie on any actor.
  Meta v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal. 2024) is the posture. Aggregate internally, purge raw items at
  thirty days, never republish a post, a handle or an image.
- *"Will this turn into an autoposter?"* — No. The room produces drafts and plans; the social
  triple-lock is untouched and nothing here can publish.
- Anti-persona: anyone wanting follower growth targets. The room does not promise reach.

## 8. Switching dynamics

Not applicable — nothing is being switched from.

## 9. Customer language

The owner's own voice belongs in `state/ventures/goviral/profile.md`, which is a form for them to
fill in rather than something to draft on their behalf. Until it is filled, the room leans on the
two magazine niches and says so in the brief.

Words to avoid in anything this room produces: "viral", "explode", "hack", "guaranteed reach", and
any number the scout data does not carry.

## 10. Brand voice

Internal and plain. It reports what the data shows, names what it does not know, and says when the
week was quiet.

## 11. Proof points

None. The venture was founded on 2026-08-06 and has held no meeting. The first brief will be the
first thing it can point at.

## 12. Goals

One brief a week, one trend snapshot a week, three ideas a week on the ledger, and two
trend-driven agendas a quarter reaching another desk. All four are counted from committed files;
none is estimated.
