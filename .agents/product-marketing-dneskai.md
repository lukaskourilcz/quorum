## 1. Product overview

A Czech daily briefing about the AI and technology stories that actually mattered. One edition a
day, written once and natively in Czech, published as static MDX at `caughtup-ai.vercel.app`.

A reader opens Today, reads the lead development, why it matters, what changed, the Briefs, the
Watchlist and the evidence, reaches **Máte přehled.** and can stop scrolling. That closing feeling
is the product.

- Category: daily news briefing. The shelf is "AI newsletter", though it is a website first.
- Type: publication. No accounts, no comments, no app.
- Business model: none active. Sponsorship is a locked hypothesis with no ad unit built.

## 2. Target audience

Busy Czech developers, founders, product and technology leaders, AI practitioners and informed
professionals. Secondary: researchers, analysts, journalists, and people who arrive through search
or the Atom feeds.

The job they hire it for: *stop me having to read twelve English newsletters to know what happened
in AI today, in my own language.*

## 3. Personas

Not B2B. One reader, one decision: open it or don't.

## 4. Problems and pain points

The volume of AI coverage exceeds what a working professional can read, most of it is in English,
and most of it is either a press release restated or a take with no evidence behind it. The cost is
either an hour a day or the quiet feeling of being behind.

## 5. Competitive landscape

- **Direct**: English AI newsletters (TLDR AI, The Rundown, Import AI). Same job, wrong language,
  and none of them has a Czech register.
- **Secondary**: Czech general-technology outlets. Right language, but AI is one beat among many
  and the depth is not there.
- **Indirect**: reading the primary sources yourself. Free, complete, and it takes the hour.

## 6. Differentiation

Written natively in Czech rather than translated — there is no translation stage in the pipeline
and none is coming back. Every claim carries its source, corrections are a public surface rather
than an edit, and the day the desk had nothing worth publishing is recorded as a no-edition day
rather than filled.

## 7. Objections and anti-personas

- *"Another AI newsletter."* — It is a website with a finishing line, not an inbox item.
- *"Machine-written."* — True, and said plainly on the page rather than hidden. Every claim is
  sourced and the provenance is published.
- Anti-persona: someone who wants the whole feed. This is the opposite of a feed.

## 8. Switching dynamics

- **Push**: twelve newsletters and no sense of completion.
- **Pull**: one edition, in Czech, that ends.
- **Habit**: the English newsletters already arrive by email.
- **Anxiety**: "will I miss the thing everyone else read?"

## 9. Customer language

Not captured. No reader research has been done and no reader has been interviewed, so there are no
verbatim phrases to record. Do not invent any — an invented quote is the one thing a magazine
about evidence cannot have.

Words to avoid: "AI-powered", "revolutionary", "game-changing", and any sentence that would survive
being written about a different product.

## 10. Brand voice

Concise, calm, direct, evidence-aware, honest about uncertainty. Never hyped. The design thesis is
"editorial intelligence presented as a precise publishing instrument, with a clear sense of
completion", and the copy is the same idea in words.

## 11. Proof points

None to cite. There is no traffic measurement, no subscriber count, no testimonial and no logo.
Reader metrics are deliberately not collected. What can be pointed at is the record itself:
published editions, their sources, and the corrections surface.

## 12. Goals

Publish reliably while widening the healthy source mix. The conversion action is a reader
returning tomorrow — which nothing currently measures, and that is a known gap rather than a
number to guess at.
